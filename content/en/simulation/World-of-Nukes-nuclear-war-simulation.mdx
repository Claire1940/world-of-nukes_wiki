<Section icon={Map} title="How the Nuclear War Simulation Works">

A serious World of Nukes nuclear war simulation is not a single explosion event. It is a multi-stage escalation model that tracks how one tactical decision cascades into planetary catastrophe. Analysts break the scenario into defined stages, each with its own targets, weapon counts, and casualty projections.

The scenario begins with a provocation: a tactical nuclear weapon used to intimidate an adversary. A single detonation of this type could kill **30,000 to 40,000 people** and send a radioactive cloud drifting across Europe. From that point, escalation follows a predictable and horrifying logic.

| Stage | Name | Trigger | Estimated Casualties |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Provocation | Tactical nuclear use to scare an opponent | 30,000-40,000 |
| 1 | Initiation | Tactical nuclear war in Europe | 3 million, then 6 million |
| 2 | Countervalue exchange | Strikes on 30 largest cities per side | 50-80 million in hours |
| 3 | Fallout and nuclear winter | Planet-wide ash and radiation | Up to 2 billion, then ~7 billion in 2 years |

<Callout type="warning" title="Important">
These figures come from simulation models and scenario analysis, not from any actual event. Real outcomes would depend on weapon counts, targeting choices, season, and weather patterns. Treat every number as an estimate range, not a certainty.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Swords} title="Stage 1: Escalation and Counterforce Strikes">

Once the nuclear threshold is crossed, the simulation moves into a tactical nuclear war in Europe. The first wave targets military capability, not cities. Missile silos, air bases, advancing troop formations, and command centers become priority targets.

In the modeled scenario, Russia delivers roughly **300 warheads** by aircraft and short-range missiles against NATO bases and forces. NATO responds with approximately **180 air-delivered warheads**. Allies on both sides enter the exchange, with North Korea and China joining the confrontation. This phase alone produces an estimated 3 million casualties.

The second counterforce wave is strategic. NATO launches about **700 warheads** from land and submarine-based missiles aimed at Russian and Chinese nuclear forces. Before losing their arsenals, both nations execute warning launches from silos, road-mobile launchers, and submarines. Casualties roughly double to 6 million.

<Callout type="tip" title="Why Infrastructure Matters">
The simulation emphasizes that destroying power generation, transmission, and government bodies can irreversibly break a state, even without massive urban casualties. Infrastructure collapse is a strategic goal, not collateral damage.

<Section icon={AlertTriangle} title="Stage 2: The War of Defeat and Extreme Scenarios">

When one side concludes that a nuclear war cannot be won, the simulation shifts from a war against strategic centers to a **war of defeat**. The objective becomes maximum damage: destroying as many cities as possible to paralyze the enemy.

Each side strikes the other's 30 most populated cities and economic hubs, using 5 to 10 warheads per city depending on population. First-hours and first-days fatalities are estimated at **50 to 80 million people**, with all major infrastructure destroyed.

The most extreme modeled scenarios include:

| Scenario | Mechanism | Projected Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep-ocean megaton detonation | Tsunami generation opposite coastal megacities | Coastal cities swept away |
| Yellowstone Caldera strike | Triggering supervolcano eruption | 1,000-2,500 cubic km of solid particles, mostly ash |
| Continental ash coverage | Ash layer up to 1 m within 2,000-3,000 km, 0.3 m elsewhere | Agriculture and industry collapse continent-wide |
| Atmospheric ash veil | Sunlight blocked for 2-4 years | Temperature drops of 3-5 degrees, possibly up to 20 |

<Callout type="info" title="Seasonal Sensitivity">
The model shows outcomes depend heavily on the time of year. A war during winter months catches flora and fauna in anabiosis in northern regions, while equatorial ecosystems, tuned to near-constant temperatures, suffer severe losses regardless of season.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Target} title="Stage 3: Fallout, Radiation Sickness, and Nuclear Winter">

Radioactive fallout spreads globally alongside volcanic and urban-fire ash. Fallout's defining danger is its ability to distribute radiation across enormous areas, affecting people, animals, and entire ecosystems far from any detonation.

High-level radiation exposure causes acute radiation sickness. Early symptoms include **nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and fatigue**. Severe cases are fatal. Survivors face long-term contamination of soil, water, and food supplies.

The final phase is nuclear winter and famine:

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Ash Veil Formation">
    Smoke and ash enter the upper atmosphere, blocking sunlight across the planet for an estimated 2-4 years. Aviation halts and global temperatures fall sharply.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Biosphere Collapse">
    Equatorial ecosystems suffer the most due to their sensitivity to temperature change. Higher life forms there may disappear almost entirely, even without direct fire damage.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Agricultural Failure">
    Industry and agriculture collapse continent-wide. Food production cannot sustain the surviving population, driving famine on every continent.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Global Die-off">
    Cumulative modeling suggests roughly 7 billion people could die within two years of a full exchange, from blast, radiation, famine, and societal collapse combined.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="The Core Conclusion">
The simulation's purpose is deterrence education, not prediction. Its final message is explicit: global cooperation to prevent nuclear war is the only rational strategy, because no nation wins a war of mutual destruction.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Key Variables That Change the Outcome">

Not every simulation run produces identical numbers. Analysts adjust several core variables, and each one can swing casualty estimates dramatically.

| Variable | Range in Models | Impact on Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warhead count per side | ~180 to 700+ per exchange wave | Scales direct casualties and infrastructure loss |
| Target list type | Counterforce vs. countervalue | Counterforce hits military; countervalue hits cities |
| Season of exchange | Winter vs. summer | Winter protects northern flora via anabiosis |
| Ash injection volume | 1,000-2,500 cubic km | Drives duration and depth of nuclear winter |
| Temperature decline | 3-5 degrees vs. up to 20 | Determines agricultural survival window |
